= Exercise 2: The built-in policy library
:navtitle: Exercise 2: Policy library

Estimated time: 5 minutes.

RHACS ships with a library of policies built from Red Hat's security expertise, industry best practices, and mappings to common compliance standards.
Every policy includes *Rationale* and *Guidance* written for developers, not just security reviewers.

== Task 2.1: Open a service-account policy

. From the left menu, navigate to *Platform Configuration → Policy Management*.
. Locate and open *Pod Service Account Token Automatically Mounted*.
. Read the *Rationale* section aloud (or to yourself).
. Point to the *Guidance* section: it tells the developer to set `automountServiceAccountToken: false` on the pod, unless that workload must call the Kubernetes API.

== Task 2.2: Lifecycle stages in the library

. Navigate back to the policy list.
. Highlight the lifecycle column — each policy shows which stage(s) it applies to at a glance.
. Note that you can write a single policy that combines criteria across stages — for example, flagging a critical CVE specifically in deployments that are also privileged.

== Key takeaways

* Every policy includes Rationale and Guidance written for developers, not just security reviewers.
* The lifecycle tags show which stage each policy applies to at a glance.
* Cross-lifecycle policy criteria eliminate the need to coordinate between separate tooling.
