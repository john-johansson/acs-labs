Estimated time: 10 minutes.

The RHACS Network Graph is three tools in one: a live traffic flow map, a firewall visualizer showing active `NetworkPolicy` rules, and an automated firewall rule builder that generates zero-trust policies from observed baselines.

In this exercise you visualize the backend and payments application topology and enable baseline violation alerting so that any new, unexpected traffic will be flagged.

*Persona: [Platform Operations]*

By the end of this exercise you will:

* Navigate the Network Graph and select the `backend` namespace.
* Identify the multi-tier application flow and cross-namespace connections.
* Open the baseline tab for a deployment and enable alerting on baseline violations.

== Task 5.1: View the backend topology

. In the RHACS Console, navigate to *Network Graph*.
. Select your cluster from the cluster dropdown.
. In the *Namespaces* list, select `backend`.
. Observe the visual graph. The expected topology is:
+
....
     [ varnish ]
          | (Port 9001)
          v
     [ api-server ] -------> [ payments/gateway ] (cross-namespace, Port 7777)
       |         \
       |          \
       v           v
 [ postgres ]   [ backend-atlas ]
 (Port 5432)    (Port 8080)
....
+
Each node represents a deployment. Lines between nodes indicate observed traffic flows. The connection from `api-server` to `payments/gateway` crosses namespace boundaries.

== Task 5.2: Inspect deployment details

. Click `postgres` in the graph to open the side panel.
. On the *Details* tab, review the *Network security* section. It shows:
** How many network flows were observed.
** Whether network policies exist that regulate ingress or egress.
** Listening ports for this deployment.
. Click the *Flows* tab to see the list of observed connections (both ingress and egress) with protocol and port details.

== Task 5.3: Enable baseline violation alerting

RHACS automatically discovers network flows during an observation period and adds them to the baseline.
After the observation period, new flows that are not in the baseline appear as anomalous.
By default, anomalous flows are visible in the graph but do not trigger violations.
Toggling *Alert on baseline violations* promotes them to policy violations.

. With `postgres` selected, click the *Baseline* tab.
. Review the listed baseline flows. You should see ingress from `api-server` on port 5432.
. Toggle *Alert on baseline violations* to the *on* position.
. Repeat for `api-server`:
.. Click `api-server` in the graph.
.. Open the *Baseline* tab.
.. Toggle *Alert on baseline violations* on.

After enabling alerts, any traffic from a source not in the baseline (such as a direct probe from another namespace) will generate a violation.

TIP: You can configure the baseline observation period by setting `ROX_NETWORK_BASELINE_OBSERVATION_PERIOD` on Central. The default is sufficient for this lab.

== Key takeaways

* The Network Graph visualizes real-time traffic flows, existing `NetworkPolicy` rules, and auto-discovered baselines in a single view.
* RHACS builds baselines automatically during the observation period. After that, unknown traffic appears as anomalous.
* Toggling *Alert on baseline violations* on the *Baseline* tab converts anomalous flows into violations, bridging from visibility to enforcement.
