Estimated time: 15 minutes.

Regulatory audits traditionally require teams to spend days aggregating spreadsheet data, zipping CSV files, and distributing them by email.
RHACS replaces that manual workflow with scheduled compliance report generation, configurable delivery destinations, and on-demand evidence downloads tied directly to scan results.

With compliance gaps identified in Exercise 2, the *Information Security* team's next step is producing the evidence package that the auditor will receive: a time-stamped, machine-generated record of every compliance control evaluated against the cluster.

By the end of this exercise you will:

* Manage scan schedule settings (frequency, profiles, clusters).
* Generate an on-demand compliance report.
* Download and review the report contents as audit evidence.
* Understand how delivery destinations automate evidence distribution in production.

== Task 3.1: Open the scan schedule detail page

. In the RHACS Console, navigate to *Compliance > OpenShift Schedules*.
. Click the `cis-audit-schedule` (created in Exercise 2) to open the detail view.
+
If you did not create it in Exercise 2, select the `acs-catch-all` schedule or the `cis-weekly-scan` schedule from Exercise 1.

== Task 3.2: Review and edit scan schedule settings

Compliance requirements change over time. Explore how to modify a scan schedule.

. On the schedule detail page, click the *Edit* button (pencil icon) or use *Actions > Edit scan schedule*.
. Review the configurable settings:
** *Name and description*: Rename for clarity as compliance programs evolve.
** *Schedule frequency*: Change from weekly to daily, monthly, or a custom cron expression.
** *Profiles*: Add or remove compliance profiles. For example, adding `ocp4-cis-node` to a schedule that only scanned `ocp4-cis`.
** *Clusters*: Expand scope as new clusters join the fleet.
. Make a small change (e.g., update the description to `Weekly CIS scan for Q4 audit`) and save.
+
Changes take effect on the next scheduled scan. Running scans are not interrupted.

. Navigate back to *Compliance > OpenShift Schedules* and verify the updated description appears.

== Task 3.3: Generate an on-demand compliance report

On-demand reports let you pull evidence at any time, outside the regular schedule.
This is useful for ad-hoc audit requests or pre-audit preparation.

. From the scan schedule detail page, open the *Actions* drop-down (upper right) and select *Generate download*.
+
You receive a confirmation that report generation has started.

. Click the *All report jobs* tab.
. Watch the job status progress through:
** *Waiting*: The job is queued.
** *Preparing*: The system is collecting and formatting compliance data.
** *Report ready for download*: The report is complete.
. Click *Ready for download* to save the file.

NOTE: Report generation typically completes in under a minute. If the job stays in *Preparing* for more than five minutes, verify that the underlying `ComplianceScan` resources have reached `DONE` (`oc get compliancescan -n openshift-compliance`).

== Task 3.4: Review the report as audit evidence

. Extract the downloaded file (it is a ZIP archive).
. Open the CSV report and review the contents. Key columns include:
** *Cluster*: Which cluster was scanned.
** *Profile*: Which compliance profile (e.g., `ocp4-cis`).
** *Check name*: The specific control evaluated.
** *Control reference*: The CIS benchmark section (e.g., 1.2.1).
** *Status*: Pass, Fail, Manual, Not Applicable, Error, or Inconsistent.
** *Rationale and remediation*: Why the control matters and how to fix failures.
+
This is the evidence an external auditor needs: a time-stamped, machine-generated record of every compliance control evaluated against the cluster.

. Find the failing check you drilled into in Exercise 2. Confirm the same status and remediation text appear in the CSV. This demonstrates that the downloadable report contains the same detail you reviewed interactively.

. Count the number of *Fail* entries and compare with the percentage shown on the Coverage page in Exercise 2. The numbers should match.

== Task 3.5: Understand delivery destinations and automation

In production, compliance reports are delivered automatically to auditors and security teams rather than downloaded manually.

. On the scan schedule detail page, look for the delivery destination configuration.
. Review how it works:
** *Email delivery*: RHACS sends reports to a distribution list on the configured schedule. This requires an SMTP integration under *Platform Configuration > Integrations > Notifiers*.
** *Download only*: Reports are generated but must be downloaded manually from the *All report jobs* tab.
+
This Showroom does not have SMTP configured, so you used the on-demand download path.
In a production environment, you would:

.. Configure an SMTP notifier under *Platform Configuration > Integrations > Email*.
.. Add the auditor distribution list (e.g., `compliance-team@example.com`) as a delivery destination on the scan schedule.
.. Set the delivery cadence to match your audit requirements (weekly, monthly, quarterly).

This eliminates the manual step entirely: auditors receive evidence automatically, and the compliance team only intervenes when failures need remediation.

== Task 3.6: Operationalizing compliance (reflection)

With scan schedules, delivery destinations, and on-demand reporting in place, consider how this fits into an ongoing compliance program:

* *Continuous visibility*: Daily or weekly scans catch configuration drift before auditors do.
* *Automated evidence delivery*: Email delivery eliminates the manual collection process entirely.
* *Trend tracking*: Comparing reports over time shows whether the compliance posture is improving or degrading.
* *Multi-cluster scale*: The same scan schedule can target multiple clusters, producing a single consolidated report.
* *Separation of duties*: Platform Operations creates scan schedules and remediates failures. Information Security receives reports and reviews evidence. Neither team needs to access the other's tools.

NOTE: Compliance reporting requires Compliance Operator version 1.6.0 or later. This environment runs version 1.10.0.

== Key takeaways

* Scan schedules in RHACS manage the full compliance lifecycle: when to scan, what to scan, and who receives the results.
* On-demand downloads handle ad-hoc audit requests. Adding email delivery destinations automates recurring evidence distribution.
* Schedule settings (frequency, profiles, clusters) can be modified at any time without disrupting running scans.
* The CSV report contains check-level detail per cluster and profile. Cross-referencing it with the Coverage page confirms that the downloadable evidence matches the interactive view.
* Operationalizing compliance means continuous scanning, automated delivery, and trend tracking, not periodic fire drills.
