= Exercise 4: Risk profiling and CVE deferral
:navtitle: Exercise 4: Risk and CVE deferral

include::partial$session2-ui.adoc[]

Estimated time: 15 minutes.

ACS computes a *Risk Priority* for each deployment from policy violations, vulnerabilities, configuration, and related signals.
Platform and security teams use that ranking to decide what to fix first, and use *vulnerability exceptions* (deferral or false positive) when a CVE cannot be fixed immediately but needs an auditable record.

By the end of this exercise you will:

* Use the Risk view to identify high-priority deployments and understand the signals that drive ranking.
* Apply a policy scope exclusion to reduce noise from non-critical namespaces.
* Review an existing vulnerability exception (deferral or false positive) and understand the audit trail.

== Task 4.1: Analyze deployment risk factors

. In the RHACS Console, open *Risk* in the left navigation.
. Sort or scan the list for a high-priority application workload.
+
On this Showroom, `visa-processor` in namespace `payments` and `log4shell` in namespace `log4shell` are reliable high-priority examples. Other candidates include `backend-atlas` (backend) and `webgoat` (webgoat).
. Click the deployment name.
. Review the risk breakdown panels:
** Policy violations (for example privileged settings or dangerous capabilities)
** Vulnerable images and CVE severity
** Configuration and exposure signals shown for that deployment
. Note one factor that would matter to platform operations and one that would matter to application owners.

== Task 4.2: Add a policy scope exclusion

Excluding non-production or sandbox workloads from a noisy policy recalibrates what appears as risk without disabling the policy globally.

. Navigate to *Platform Configuration* > *Policy Management*.
. Open *Fixable Severity at least Important* (enabled by default on this cluster).
. Click *Actions* > *Edit policy*.
. In the policy wizard, navigate to the *Policy scope* step.
. Under *Exclude by scope*, click *Add exclusion scope*.
. Set *Namespace* to a namespace you are willing to carve out of this policy for the demo.
+
Example: namespace `frontend` (or another non-critical app namespace in your environment).
. Save the policy.
. Return to *Risk* and refresh the view.
. Confirm that the excluded namespace's deployments are no longer driven by that policy's findings the same way (violations for that policy should stop accumulating there).

IMPORTANT: After the exercise, remove the exclusion (or set the policy back to its previous scope) so later labs and other students are not affected.

== Task 4.3: Review vulnerability exceptions (deferral and false positive)

Creating a brand-new deferral request can fail on some Showroom builds even for Admin.
This task focuses on the auditable exception record that security and platform teams rely on.

. In the RHACS Console, expand *Vulnerability Management* in the left navigation and open *Exception Management*.
. Find an existing exception on this cluster. Sample records often include:
** A *Deferred* exception for `CVE-2021-44228` (Log4Shell)
** A *False positive* exception for another CVE
. For one exception, note:
** Target state (`Deferred` or `False positive`)
** Status (`Approved`)
** Scope (image registry, remote, and tag pattern)
** Approver or requester identity if shown
. Discuss with the group: what rationale would you require before approving a 30-day deferral in production?

OPTIONAL: If your UI offers *Defer* or *Request deferral* on a CVE and the request succeeds, complete it with a 30-day duration and rationale
`Lab deferral: vendor fix under evaluation; compensating controls documented for security review.`
If the request fails or never creates a record, continue with the existing Approved exceptions above. That still meets the learning goal for this lab.

== Cleanup

NOTE: On a single-use Showroom that is destroyed after the session, you can skip cleanup. On a shared or reusable cluster, remove the namespace exclusion from *Fixable Severity at least Important* so later labs and other students are not affected.

== Key takeaways

* *Risk* (left navigation) ranks deployments by aggregating policy violations, vulnerabilities, configuration, and exposure signals. Open a deployment to see the breakdown.
* Policy scope exclusions (under *Policy scope* > *Exclude by scope*) keep production gates strict while reducing noise from sandboxes.
* Approved deferral and false-positive exceptions under *Vulnerability Management* > *Exception Management* are the auditable security record when a CVE is not fixed immediately.
